AmpFootballFit: exercise review sheet for physios and coaches

App version 4.3, 20 November 2026 · Alexander Craens, initiator of AmpFootballFit

What I am asking

I am asking you to read the exercise list below and tell me which exercises you would change, remove or add, by replying to ampfootballfit@tutanota.com.

AmpFootballFit is a free app for amputee footballers (crutch-based, single-leg support). It has 138 exercises in 26 progression groups, plus exercises outside a tier group. You can review only the groups that fall in your own field, for example calf work, balance or upper body. A review of one group is useful.

  • Open the app: https://acraens.github.io/AmpFootballFit/
  • Reply to: ampfootballfit@tutanota.com

What the app does

The app is a 12-week return-to-play program in 3 phases of 4 weeks each: Base (weeks 1 to 4), Load Progression (weeks 5 to 8) and Sport Transfer (weeks 9 to 12). The player logs sessions, answers a daily readiness check and can share a plain-text summary with a coach or physio.

Most strength exercises sit in a progression group with up to 4 tiers, from easiest (tier 1) to hardest (tier 4). 18 exercises are marked as advanced. Before an advanced exercise joins a player's sessions, a pop-up says these exercises add load, explosive movement or change of direction, and are meant for athletes already training at full capacity.

Every rule in the app is advisory: it warns the player and never blocks them. The app does not teach football technique; its Skills tab is empty on purpose.

This sheet describes app version 4.3 as live on 20 November 2026. One physiotherapist has reviewed the program so far (feedback applied 19 November 2026).

Cardio

TierPhaseExercisePrescription in the appYour review
-1Low-intensity steady-state15–25 min continuous at an easy, conversational effort (RPE 3–4/10, you should be able to speak in full sentences throughout). Use whatever you have access to: rowing machine, arm/upper-body ergometer, stationary bike, brisk crutch-walk, swimming, or wheelchair/handcycle. Any continuous activity you can sustain at that effort works. Rowing is the best choice: it trains the upper and lower body together, as in a match. Longer walks on crutches, including stairs, also count.
-2Interval work30s work / 30s rest or 20s work / 40s rest, 6–10 rounds, building toward match-intensity bursts. "Work" = whichever hard-effort mode you train with (crutch sprints, bike, rowing/arm ergometer, or shuttle runs) at a hard effort (RPE 7–8/10).
-3Repeated-sprint-style intervalsSuggested starting structure: 6–10s max-effort burst (on crutches, or your usual match-day mobility method), 30–60s recovery, 6–8 reps. Adjust based on observed match demand.
1 (advanced)3Acceleration over 10 m, controlled effort3–4 sets x 10 m. Each set is one acceleration over 10 m at about 70% effort. Walk back to recover before the next set.
2 (advanced)3Acceleration over 10–20 m, hard effort4–5 sets x 10–20 m. Each set is one acceleration from a standing start at about 85% effort. Walk back to recover before the next set.
3 (advanced)3Sprint mechanics / acceleration4–6 sets x 10–20 m. Each set is one upright sprint acceleration from a standing start. Take a full recovery between sets.

How to reply and what happens next

Reply by email to ampfootballfit@tutanota.com. A marked-up printout or a photo of it is fine.

For an existing exercise, give its name and one verdict: keep, change or remove. For a change, add your wording or numbers.

For a new exercise, give:

  1. Name and what it trains
  2. Prescription (sets and reps, or seconds)
  3. An easier version
  4. Who should not do it

The developer applies accepted changes and publishes them at the same link. Players on the browser version get them without updating anything.
